# Instagram caption — "Your AI chat isn't a diary"

**She used an AI chatbot like a diary. Then deputies showed up at her door.** 🔦📓

This is the week's most-read AI privacy story, so here is exactly what has been reported, and what hasn't. (Everything below is "alleged": a charge is not a conviction.)

📅 **What's reported:** On September 26, a Florida woman allegedly wrote in a Claude chat that she'd "shoot up" a sheriff's office. A message the next day allegedly mentioned a new gun.

🔍 **How it reached police:** Anthropic says it monitors chats for threatening content. Serious flags can go to a human reviewer. In this case the team decided to report it to law enforcement.

⚖️ **The charge:** Court records list a felony charge dated September 30 under Florida Statute 836.10 (a written threat of a mass shooting). Arraignment is set for November 2. The arrest report isn't in the court file yet.

📈 **Not the first:** Reports say at least two other chats reached police since August: San Antonio on Aug 11 (a 22-year-old man arrested) and San Francisco on Aug 14 (no arrest or charges).

🏢 **Not just one company:** OpenAI has said chats about harming others can go to human reviewers, and imminent threats of serious harm may be referred to police.

📜 **The fine print:** Anthropic's privacy policy (effective Sept 10) allows disclosure when it has a good-faith belief it's "reasonably necessary to … prevent serious harm to any person or to property."

🧮 **The gap:** Its transparency report lists zero emergency requests from police in the second half of 2025, but it doesn't count reports the company makes on its own.

🗣️ **The sheriff's line:** What you share with AI is "never truly anonymous." What we don't know: how often chats get flagged, the exact messages, or what the arrest report will say.
