## 1. Storytelling gate (docs/STORYTELLING-GUIDE.md)
- **Protagonist:** Prime Focus / DNEG and its founder-CEO Namit Malhotra, told as a company, not a person. **Obstacle:** a service business with huge fixed costs that nearly stalled when Hollywood stopped shooting (2023–24). **Reversal:** instead of cutting, it doubled down and became the *owner* of a film instead of a vendor; the film then earns record trade-estimated gross, **but gross is not cash in the producer's hand and the parent still carries ~₹5,000 cr of debt (reported)**. **Honest ending:** Part 2 (reported Diwali 2027) will show whether the model works; the video lists three numbers to watch, not a verdict.
- **One-sentence test:** "A VFX company that started in a garage and won eight Oscars bet its future on owning a film, and the box office is huge, but what the company keeps, and when, is what decides whether the bet pays."
- **But/Therefore chain:** garage loan → BUT the 2008 crash → THEREFORE 3D conversion kept it alive → merger with Double Negative → Oscars → BUT a factory whose biggest cost is people → THEREFORE it needs a steady order book → BUT the 2023–24 strikes empty the bench → THEREFORE it bets on its own film → BUT the last big-VFX Ramayana film (2023) was a cautionary case → THEREFORE this one is built on cameras, IMAX and numbers → release → THE NUMBER (gross vs budget vs debt) → BUT gross ≠ cash → twist: the hero is also a shareholder and the market priced the win before release → full circle to the garage.
- **Open-loop ledger:** (1) "ek number jo sab kuch badal deta hai" planted ~0:50 (B03) → paid 8:06 (B18); (2) "garage se IMAX tak" planted ~0:50 → paid 10:10 (B22); (3) "Part 2 ke liye kya dekhein" planted 8:40 (B19b) → paid 10:32 (B23).
- **Cold-read review:** not done (creator declined earlier ones); the mute test is in §8.

## 2. Style record (Lemo-Opuscar)
- **Style:** `bauhaus-shapes` (read in full 21 Nov 2026), runner-up `data-physical`. Topic type "finance & mechanisms with no characters": every quantity is a shape and **area is the amount** (budget, gross, debt, Oscars), so the central argument ("gross vs budget vs debt") is something you *see* to scale, and no human likenesses are needed (no film cast ever appears as art).
- **Invariants kept:** (1) shapes are amounts: areas strictly proportional to the stated numbers, conflicting numbers shown as two overlapping bands, never one invented figure; (2) shapes have roles (circle = what grows/moves, square = what weighs, triangle = pivot/time) and one colour per role; (3) a visible construction grid that lays out each scene and retracts (nothing fades), type only names things and arrives by rising from a slot.
- **What we adapt (our rules win):** the style is 2D flat; ours is **Blender 3D**: the shapes are thick matte slabs and discs on a paper-coloured ground, lit by one soft key, with a real authored camera (dolly, crane, orbit, push-through-a-colour-band) and depth of field; no gradients on shapes; paper tooth as a texture; subtitles bottom-right; 30 fps; 1920×1080; text through `scripts/blender_text.py` (crisp overlay pass at final resolution).
- **Palette (new for the channel, one role per colour):** paper `#F3F0E8`, protagonist vermilion `#E8553D` (the bet / Prime Focus), field deep teal `#0C6B58` (box office / revenue), pivot marigold `#F2B632` (Oscars / DNEG), secondary sand `#D9D3C3`, ink blue-black `#1B1E2B` (type and thin lines only), plus **one extra role colour, oxblood `#8E1F2F`, for debt** (the only deviation: debt must read as the opposite of revenue). Fonts: Jost 800/300 (to vendor, OFL) + IBM Plex Mono (labels, source tags). English only on screen.
- **Why not the reference video's 2D collage grammar:** our standing rules (Blender-first, cinematic 3D, no cartoon people, real photos only when licensed) replace it; what we *keep* from it is the grammar (below).
