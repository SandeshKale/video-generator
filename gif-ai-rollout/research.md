# LinkedIn GIF #: "Everyone got the same AI. Only some teams lit up." (research, 2026-10-15)

## Why this topic
Picked after a second pass over candidates (see `../gif-next-research/research.md`). The criteria: relatable to anyone who has been through an AI rollout, a **primary source read today**, hard numbers, and a message that is useful, not scary. The earlier candidates were rejected for weak sourcing (AI "intensifies" work: HBR paywalled, secondary coverage only; bot-applicant arms race: one March article; Microsoft "infinite workday": a 2025 report, and the 2026 numbers I found were secondary blogs).

## Source (tier A, read in full on 15 Oct 2026)
Gallup, "Employee Engagement Remains Flat as AI Adoption Accelerates", Jim Harter, 21 July 2026. Surveys of Gallup Panel members, field periods 4-19 Feb and 6-20 May 2026, 43,262 US adults working full or part time (±0.7 pts); AI-integrated subsample 21,724 (±1.0 pts).

Facts used (all from that page):
- 31% of US employees engaged in H1 2026, 18% actively disengaged, both unchanged from 2025. Engagement peaked at 36% in 2020 and fell to 31% in 2024.
- Each point is about 1.6 million US workers; 8 million fewer engaged employees than in 2020.
- Not-engaged and actively disengaged employees cost the US economy an estimated $2 trillion a year in lost productivity (Gallup's estimate).
- Manager actively supports team's AI use: **48%** engaged vs **30%** for those who say they do not (18 points).
- Organisation has a clear AI integration plan: **15 points** more engaged than without one.
- Within AI-adopting organisations, weekly-or-more AI users are **8 points** more engaged than less frequent users.
- With frequent use, a clear plan and manager support all present: **53%** engaged.
- Employees at AI-adopting organisations are 6 points more engaged than at non-adopters (not used on the GIF).
- Top rating for AI's productivity impact (weekly+ users in AI-adopting orgs): 9% without active manager support vs 33% with it. (50% when all supportive conditions are present **and the employee is engaged**, so that figure is not used.)
- Quotes: "Making AI available is not the same as having a clear AI implementation plan." / "Employees are substantially more likely to be engaged when leaders introduce AI with clear expectations, a thoughtful implementation plan and active manager support."

## Caveats to state
- Correlational survey data: engaged people may also be more likely to report supportive managers. Gallup does not claim causation on the page.
- The three towers are subgroups (manager does not / does back AI use; all three conditions), not three companies, and the lever bars are separate comparisons, not additive. 8 + 15 + 18 is not 53 − 30.
- 1 window = 1 in 100 employees is a drawing convention.
- Disclosure: this is analysis of a Gallup publication; Claude helped draft the post and build the animation. The topic touches AI vendors only indirectly.

## Metaphor (chosen; the creator asked for "the next one, unmistakably better")
Three identical office towers at night-time-in-day: each window is one employee in 100; a lit window is an engaged employee. Same AI in all three. A breaker panel of three switches (manager backs it, clear plan, weekly use) turns the lights on: 30 lit, then 48, then 53. Moving parts: 300 windows lighting in sequence, three rolling counters, three toggle switches with springs, bars, lime outline glow, confetti burst, drifting clouds, cars on the street, blinking antennas, a scrolling fact ticker.
