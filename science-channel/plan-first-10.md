## Part 1: The series system (identical in every reel)

**Promise (bio, badge, caption line 1):** *"What's really going on inside you and the stuff around you, in 30 seconds."*

| Element | Rule |
|---|---|
| Length | **25–30 s**, 60–80 spoken words, one idea. |
| Frame 0 | Already moving: the hero object (or your face, for test B), plus **≤5 words about "you"** in the top third (y 200–520). Never a logo or title card. Voice starts at **0.00 s**. |
| By 1.5 s | The surprising number or claim is on screen. |
| Signature object | **The X-ray lens**: a circular cut-away lens that sweeps over the object and shows its inside. Every reel uses it at least once; it doubles as the match-cut tool between beats, like the cable in the trading-pipeline reel. |
| Signature data object | **The zoom ruler**: a vertical scale bar, bottom left (y ≤ 1500), showing the current scale (1 m → 1 cm → 1 µm → 1 nm, or km → AU for space). It moves with every zoom, so the viewer always knows how deep they are. |
| Meaning-coded palette | Deep ink ground. **X-ray cyan** = hidden/inside. **Signal coral** = the surprising number. **Lime** = "try this yourself". **Amber** = "scientists disagree / caveat". Same mapping in every reel. Exact hex values are fixed at the mockup. |
| Type | One display face for headlines, one mono face for numbers and labels, and Inter for captions. Pick fonts not used by recent reels and vendor them per CLAUDE.md. |
| Caveat chip | Any contested claim gets an amber `SCIENTISTS DISAGREE` or `IN MICE` chip on screen. This channel's credibility is the moat. |
| Share line at ~70% | One specific line: "Send this to the friend who…" Written fresh per reel (Part 2). |
| End (last 2 s) | Series badge, a next-episode tease in ≤5 words, and FOLLOW. The last frame visually matches frame 0 so the loop is seamless and replays count. |
| Captions | Word-highlighted, ≤2 lines, on a dark pill, in the caption band. No other content is allowed there. |
| Instagram caption | 300–700 characters: the hook question on line 1, 3–4 lines of the answer, a "Source:" line with the paper, the AI-voice disclosure line, and **5 hashtags** (topic, intent, audience, niche, series `#WhatsActuallyHappening`). |
| Safe zone | All CLAUDE.md rules: content inside x 150–918 and y 140–1536, plus `check.mjs`, `visual-validate.mjs` and `change-cadence.mjs` (something changes every ≤2 s). |

Mockup first, per CLAUDE.md: build the series frame once, with 3 scenes from 3 different reels (one body, one tech, one space), in 3 asset families. Approve it before any full build.

## Part 2: The 10 reels

The posting order puts the highest-share topic first and spaces out the two tests. "Jev" is the topic score: 35% send, 30% scroll-stop, 20% visual payoff, 15% surprise, on a 0–4 scale. Jev's scores sit close together (1.43–1.89), so they only broke ties. The topics were chosen for variety and verified facts. Fact tiers: **A** = peer-reviewed paper or agency source, **B** = established textbook fact, **C** = don't say.

### #01 · Your voice on recordings (body/senses · Jev 1.75, highest send score 0.97)
- **Full script:** `science-channel/reel-01-your-voice/script.md`.
- **Frame-0 text:** "You've never heard YOU"
- **Hook line:** "You've never heard your own voice the way everyone else does."
- **Beats:**
  - 0–10 s: 3D head cut-away. Two routes to your ears: **air** (cyan, what everyone hears) and **bone** (coral, only you). A recording only catches the air.
  - 10–18 s: "higher or lower?" One theory says a recording should sound higher. In a 2025 study most people said lower. Scientists still disagree (amber chip).
  - 18–28 s: share line, then a try-it: plug your ears and talk, with the audio mix going muffled to match. Last line: "Nobody else will ever hear the version you hear."
- **Facts:** two routes, and a recording drops the bone route (A, Hullar, Scientific American). Earplugs leave only the bone route (A, same source). The strong bone-conduction theory predicts "higher" (A, Walter et al. 2025, *Canadian Acoustics*). Most participants judged their recording *lower* (A, same abstract).
  - **Correction (13 Oct):** the first draft said bone conduction makes you sound deeper in your head and a recording thinner and higher. The sources disagree on direction, so the reel makes no direction claim and uses the disagreement as the twist.
  - Don't claim exact Hz bands. Microphones and speakers also colour the sound a little (Frontiers for Young Minds 2025).
- **Share line:** "Send this to the friend who hates voice notes."

### #02 · You make ~4 million new cells every second (body · Jev 1.89)
- **Frame-0 text:** "4 million. Every second."
- **Hook line:** "In the time you read this, your body made about four million new cells."
- **Beats:**
  - A coral counter ticks at 3.8 M/s through the whole reel.
  - Zoom ruler: 1 m to 10 µm into the bloodstream.
  - A count ring: **about 86% by count are blood cells** (corrected 14 Oct 2026 from the paper's results sentence; its abstract says "close to 90%"). Full brief: `reel-02-cells/script.md`.
  - Myth chip: "a new body every 7 years" ✗.
  - Caveat: most of your brain's neurons stay with you for life.
- **Facts:** about 330 billion cells turned over per day, about 30 trillion cells in the body, about 86% of the daily count is blood cells (**A**, Sender & Milo 2021, *Nature Medicine* 27:45–48). 3.8 M/s is derived (330 bn ÷ 86,400 s), so say "about 4 million".
- **Share line:** "Send this to the friend who says they're a new person now."

### #03 · You're barely outnumbered by bacteria (body · Jev 1.85)
- **Frame-0 text:** "The 10-to-1 thing is wrong"
- **Hook line:** "You've heard bacteria outnumber your cells ten to one. It's closer to one to one."
- **Beats:**
  - Two crowds fill a balance scale: about 38 trillion bacteria vs about 30 trillion human cells, ratio about **1.3 : 1**.
  - A stamp crosses out 10:1.
  - Mass reveal: all those bacteria weigh **about 0.2 kg**.
  - Closer, using the paper's own words: each trip to the toilet "may flip the ratio" in favour of human cells.
- **Facts:** **A**, Sender, Fuchs & Milo 2016, *PLOS Biology*, "Revised estimates for the number of human and bacteria cells in the body". The flip line is a direct paraphrase of the paper's discussion.
- **Share line:** "Send this to the friend who still quotes ten to one."

### #04 · The sunlight on your face is ancient (space · Jev 1.87) · test A: cosmic
- **Frame-0 text:** "This light is ancient"
- **Hook line:** "The sunlight on your skin left the Sun eight minutes ago. Its energy started out long before that."
- **Beats:**
  - A Blender cut-away of the Sun.
  - The energy's random zigzag through the dense interior, with a year counter racing into the tens of thousands.
  - Breakout at the surface, then a straight 8 min 20 s dash to Earth with a timer.
  - Zoom ruler in km, then AU.
  - Closer: "older than farming" (agriculture is about 12,000 years old).
- **Facts:**
  - 8 min 20 s Sun to Earth (B).
  - NASA Goddard: energy takes "tens of thousands of years" to zigzag out (A, NASA SVS 11084).
  - Model estimates of 100,000–170,000 years exist, but say "tens of thousands of years, by NASA's estimate". Add a caveat chip: it's energy re-emitted many times, not one photon.
- **Share line:** "Send this to the friend who's always 'five minutes away'."

### #05 · There's a hole in your vision (brain · Jev 1.76) · test B: face open · interactive
- **Frame-0 text:** "There's a hole in your vision"
- **Hook line:** "Close your left eye. One of these two dots is about to vanish."
- **Beats:**
  - A cross and a dot placed inside the safe zone.
  - Instruction: stare at the cross with your right eye and bring the phone slowly closer.
  - Reveal: the optic nerve leaves the eye where there are no light sensors.
  - The brain paints over the gap with a guess (the X-ray lens shows the retina).
- **Facts:** blind spot about 7.5° across, about 15° to the temple side of fixation (**A/B**, University of Iowa EyeRounds visual-field tutorial).
- **⚠ Must be tested on 2–3 real phones** during the mockup. At about 18–20 cm the 15° gap fits inside the 768 px safe width; adjust the dot positions until it works.
- **Share line:** "Send this to someone and watch them try it."

### #06 · Goosebumps have a second job (body · Jev 1.89) · test B: face open
- **Frame-0 text:** "Goosebumps aren't useless"
- **Hook line:** "Goosebumps look pointless. The nerve behind them may also be waking up your hair."
- **Beats:**
  - 3D skin cross-section. The arrector pili muscle contracts and the hair stands.
  - The sympathetic nerve lights up, connects to the hair-follicle stem cells and wakes them.
  - Long-term cold leads to new hair growth.
  - Amber `IN MICE` chip.
- **Facts:** **A**, Shwartz et al. 2020, *Cell* 182(3):578–593 (Harvard/Hsu lab). The muscle and nerve form a niche that regulates hair-follicle stem cells. Done in mice, so it must be said on screen.
- **Share line:** "Send this to the friend who's always cold."

### #07 · Wrinkly fingers aren't water soaking in (body · Jev 1.85) · test B: face open
- **Frame-0 text:** "Not water. Your nerves."
- **Hook line:** "Pruney fingers aren't water soaking in. Your nerves are doing it."
- **Beats:**
  - Finger cross-section: blood vessels narrow (sympathetic vasoconstriction) and the skin buckles into treads.
  - Proof: a finger with a cut nerve stays smooth, which is why doctors use it as a nerve test.
  - Match cut to a tyre tread: the "rain tread" idea. Wet marbles moved 12% faster.
  - Amber stamp: `2014: COULDN'T REPRODUCE`.
- **Facts:**
  - Denervated fingers don't wrinkle; used clinically as the "wrinkle test" (A/B, Lewis & Pickering 1935; 1977 study of 41 nerve-injury patients).
  - The mechanism is vasoconstriction (A, Wilder-Smith & Chow 2003).
  - Grip benefit: 12% faster with wet objects (A, Kareklas et al. 2013, *Biology Letters*), not reproduced (A, Haseleu et al. 2014, *PLOS ONE*).
- **Share line:** "Send this to the friend who stays in the shower for an hour."

### #08 · Your touchscreen feels electricity, not pressure (tech · Jev 1.79)
- **Frame-0 text:** "Your screen can't feel you"
- **Hook line:** "Your phone screen doesn't feel your touch. It feels the electricity in your finger."
- **Beats:**
  - An exploded 3D phone: glass, then the electrode grid, then the display.
  - The field lines bend at the fingertip because your skin conducts and changes the grid's capacitance.
  - A wool glove blocks it (insulator).
  - A conductive-thread glove lights up.
  - Lime "try it": the knuckle works, a plastic pen doesn't.
- **Facts:** projected-capacitive screens sense a change in capacitance from a conductive object, and insulating gloves block it (B, standard engineering).
  - Avoid the "sausage stylus" anecdote unless it's verified first.
- **Share line:** "Send this to the friend who takes their gloves off to text."

### #09 · About 90% of your weight was made inside stars (space · Jev 1.76) · test A: cosmic
- **Frame-0 text:** "90% of you: made in stars"
- **Hook line:** "Almost everything you weigh was made inside a star."
- **Beats:**
  - A body silhouette fills with element tiles coloured by origin: hydrogen from the Big Bang (about 10% of body mass); oxygen and carbon from stars.
  - Calcium, iron and phosphorus from dying and exploding stars.
  - Closer: SDSS/APOGEE mapped these "elements of life" across 150,000 stars in our galaxy.
- **Facts:**
  - Body mass is about 61–65% oxygen, 18–23% carbon, about 10% hydrogen and about 3% nitrogen (B, ICRP reference values).
  - Hydrogen is primordial, and the heavier elements come from stellar nucleosynthesis (B).
  - The APOGEE 2017 study covered CHNOPS, which make up over 97% of body mass (A, SDSS press release).
  - **Don't say** "we share 97% of our atoms with the galaxy" (it's a common misreading).
- **Share line:** "Send this to someone who needs to feel cosmic today."

### #10 · Hiccups may be left over from our water-breathing ancestors (body/evolution · Jev 1.85)
- **Frame-0 text:** "Hiccups: a fish leftover?"
- **Hook line:** "Your hiccups might be a breathing reflex left over from ancestors that used gills."
- **Beats:**
  - A side-by-side split: the human diaphragm jerks and the glottis snaps shut, next to a tadpole gill pump. The two rhythms sync.
  - Shared clues: the glottis closes while breathing in, and both are suppressed by more CO₂. That is why holding your breath sometimes works.
  - Amber chip `ONE LEADING HYPOTHESIS`.
- **Facts:** **A**, Straus et al. 2003, *BioEssays* 25:182–188: the phylogenetic hypothesis, glottal closure during inspiration, inhibition by raised CO₂.
  - The often-quoted "35 ms" figure is from a different paper (Howes), so leave it out unless that paper is read.
- **Share line:** "Send this to the friend who hiccups at everything."
