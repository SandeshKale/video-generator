## 1. Caption (paste as one block)

```
About four million of your cells are replaced every second. 🧬
That is roughly 330 billion a day. About 86% of them are blood cells, and a red blood cell lasts about four months. The lining of your gut turns over in 3 to 5 days. And the idea of a brand-new body every seven years? Not quite: most of your brain's neurons are never replaced.
The counter in the video runs at the paper's average rate for a reference adult (healthy, 70 kg, aged 20 to 30). Real rates vary with age, sex and health.
```

## 6. Fact tiers (see `script.md` ledger)
All A (read first-hand in the full text of Sender & Milo 2021): 330 +/- 20 billion cells per day, about 4 million per second; about 86% blood cells; red-cell lifespan 110-120 days; gut epithelia 3-5 days; most brain neurons and eye-lens cells are not replaced and complete turnover never happens. The counter is derived (330 bn / 86,400 s = 3.82 M/s) and labelled as an average. Nothing here claims the rate for an individual. The source of the "new body every seven years" idea was not traced, so it is only "the idea", never attributed.
