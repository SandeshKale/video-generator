## Recommendation: #1 Pala, with #2 Lightning as the spring follow-up
**Why pala first:** it is the only topic with a date. Rabi sowing is over, cold waves run through December and January, and a reel about a danger is only useful *before* it. It also has the cleanest "I can check this tonight" take-away.

### One-sentence story
On a clear, still winter night the leaf in your field is colder than the air a thermometer reads, so the pala can freeze your crop while the weather says it is above zero.

### Beat sheet sketch (But / Therefore), 70–75 s, Hinglish
1. **Hook:** "Thermometer ne chaar degree dikhaya. Subah khet mein pala pada tha." (a field at dawn, white)
2. **Plant:** the crops at risk (potato, mustard, lentil) and what a bad night costs (reported Punjab/Bihar figures, labelled REPORTED).
3. **BUT:** the thermometer measures the air about chest height; the leaf sits at the ground.
4. **THEREFORE (the physics, built step by step):** by day the leaf takes heat from the Sun; at night it sends that heat out to the sky; a cloud or fog or a cover sends part of it back; a clear sky does not. Low, open fields lose the most (CEED analysis).
5. **Dark moment:** a clear, windless night after a warm day is the worst case; show the same field on a cloudy night staying warm.
6. **Turn: what helps, ranked by evidence.** To be confirmed from Indian primary sources before anything is said: a full straw or cloth cover helps, wetting the soil is "contested", smoke has no demonstrated benefit. This beat is the sensitive one (it contradicts a village habit), so it ships only with an ICAR or state agricultural university source, and it is worded as "kya kaam karta hai" not "aap galat hain".
7. **Take-away and follow ask:** the two-thermometer test, then Mausam/Meghdoot (IMD + ICAR block-level forecast and advisories) as the place to check the night's forecast.
8. **Last payoff:** the dawn field again, now explained.

### Camera ideas (people-free)
Low tracking move over wheat rows at blue hour (locked horizon) → push into one leaf at macro scale (rack focus from leaf to the starry sky above it, "the sky is what the leaf talks to") → thermal-vision match cut showing the leaf colder than the air at chest height (a labelled illustration, not a measurement) → crane up to show the low field filling with cold air and mist → dawn reveal.
