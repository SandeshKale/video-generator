# LinkedIn copy: "AI doesn't cut the work. It grows it." (vertical GIF)

## Post (recommended)

Everyone expects AI to give them their time back.

The research keeps saying otherwise.

A UC Berkeley team (published in Harvard Business Review) followed 40 people at a 200-person tech company from April to December 2025. Their finding: AI didn't shrink the workday. People worked faster, took on a wider scope of tasks, and let work leak into breaks, evenings and early mornings.

An Upwork survey of about 2,500 workers (2024) found 77% say AI has added to their workload, with many spending extra time reviewing AI output.

It's an old pattern. In 1865 William Stanley Jevons noticed that more efficient steam engines meant more coal burned, not less. Cheaper work gets demanded more, not less.

So the saved hours get spent, quietly. A report that took eight hours now takes two, and the next four reports are already on your desk (that part of my graphic is illustrative).

What I'm trying to do about it:
- decide what the saved time is for before the day starts
- keep one real pause in the middle of the day
- say no to scope that appears just because "AI can do it fast"

Has AI given you time back at work, or just raised the bar? 👇
