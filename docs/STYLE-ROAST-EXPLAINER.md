# House style: "Roasted & Explained" (sarcastic roast explainers)

Built 18 Oct 2026 from five research briefs: ours (`docs/COMEDY-EXPLAINER-RESEARCH.md`) and four supplied by the creator (the "Roasting God's design choices" framework, the "Explaining anything like it insulted you" brief, the creator-reference brief, and the "Pedagogy of Ridicule" paper). Part A is the analysis of the five sources; Part B is the style we use; Part C is the production kit; Part D is a worked example.

---

## Part A: analysis of the five briefs

### A1. Where all five agree (keep these, they are the spine)
1. **Roast the concept, the system and ourselves, never the viewer or vulnerable people** (punch up or sideways).
2. **The joke must be built from the mechanism** ("integrated", "the punchline is the explanation", "fact-in-the-punchline"), not stuck next to it.
3. **Facts stay 100% accurate.** The joke may exaggerate; the fact may not. If you remove the jokes, the explanation must still stand.
4. **Deadpan delivery**: the calm, unimpressed voice makes the absurd land and keeps dark material "benign".
5. **End warm** (the "toast"): one sincere line after the roast.
6. **Callbacks**: an early joke returns at the end, now understood more deeply.

### A2. What each brief adds that the others lack
| Brief | Best idea we adopt |
|---|---|
| Ours | Alternate plain fact and joke (the humour-memory effect only works in mixed lists); a "REAL FACT" stamp against deadpan misreading; "send it to a friend" CTA (sends drive reach); India-specific legal guardrails; mapping to repo assets. |
| "Roasting God's design choices" | The **5-beat Roast & Toast** shape, especially **Beat 4 "the dark turn"** ("haha… wait… oh no") followed by **Beat 5 "the toast"**; the tone dial from gentle sarcasm to full existential roast. |
| "Explaining anything like it insulted you" | The **4-layer transform** (textbook → roast → visual metaphor → actual lesson → closing punchline); four asset classes (metaphor, scene, explanatory, comedy); "verify the mechanism first, then invent the visual". |
| Creator-reference brief | The reference map (Casually Explained deadpan, Half as Interesting footnote-roasting, Oversimplified running gags, Behind the Bastards rigour + coping humour); **self-deprecation** to soften roasts; **visual punchlines** (labels and "true story" tags that land a joke without words). |
| "Pedagogy of Ridicule" | The **Vampire Effect** (a joke so strong it eats the fact); **Cunk-style feigned ignorance** (claim something wrong with confidence, then correct it); **gentle vs harsh satire** (harsh satire and attacks on the audience lose trust); **joke placement clusters** at the open, middle and close. |

### A3. Claims that are overstated or wrong (do not repeat them)
| Claim | Verdict | What the evidence says |
|---|---|---|
| "Nobody remembers the textbook, everybody remembers the insult"; "the joke IS the explanation" | Overstated | Humour helps attention and memory **of the joke**; learning gains are mixed. In the BJEP "Punchline with(out) purpose" work, on-topic humour learned about the **same** as no humour, while off-topic humour scored 7–9% **lower** ([summary](https://www.learningandthebrain.com/blog/do-classroom-jokes-help-students-learn/)). **Humour buys watch time and shares; the fact still has to be said plainly.** |
| BVT humour "lowers cortisol" and "activates dopamine" so memory is "primed" (Pedagogy of Ridicule) | Unsupported as written | The benign violation theory is well supported for *what is funny* ([McGraw lab](https://leeds-faculty.colorado.edu/mcgrawp/pdf/Benign_Violation_Theory.html)); the cortisol/dopamine learning chain is not established by the cited sources. Don't use it as a claim. |
| Vampire Effect is "validated in educational media" | Partly | It is a real advertising finding: humour reduced brand and product recall, **more so for low-involvement viewers** (Dobbelstein, Oberluggauer & Mason 2025, n = 443) ([DUT thesis](https://ir.dut.ac.za/handle/10321/5776)). Feed scrollers are low-involvement, so the risk applies to us. |
| Joke timing advice | Real, and sobering | 531 talks at 14 conferences: jokes cluster at the start and end with a mid bump; **66% got only polite chuckles, ~9% got whole-room laughter** ([Mammola et al. 2026, Proc. R. Soc. B](https://www.utupub.fi/items/287444e1-2577-46c0-855d-308ac18423a2/full)). Write more jokes than you need and cut the weak ones. |
| Example: wolves "changing rivers" (trophic cascade) | **Disputed science** | The 2014 viral claim is contested; later studies question the river change and the tall-aspen sampling ([Wolf Conservation Center](https://wolf.org/wolf-info/international-wolf-magazine/do-wolves-really-change-rivers-2/), [Discover Wildlife](https://www.discoverwildlife.com/wildlife/mammals/yellowstone-wolf-reintroduction)). A comedy reel must not launder a myth. |
| Example: apoptosis as "cellular suicide… drinking cyanide" | **Banned** | Suicide/self-harm humour: Instagram removes graphic fictional depictions incl. memes and keeps related content out of Explore ([Meta, 2019](https://about.fb.com/news/2019/10/taking-more-steps-to-keep-the-people-who-use-instagram-safe/)). Also harmful. Use "the cell's planned retirement" instead. |
| Example: garbage collection "incinerates anything you haven't touched for five seconds" | Wrong | GC frees memory that is *unreachable*, not *unused for a while*. The roast must follow the real rule. |
| Example: quantum superposition "until an observer barges in" | Misleading | "Observer" means measurement/interaction, not a person looking. Classic myth. |
| Example: Newton "hit by an apple" | Myth | Newton described seeing an apple fall; the bonk on the head is legend. |
| Example: messy room = Second Law of Thermodynamics | Loose analogy | Fine only if labelled as an analogy; it is not literal thermodynamic entropy. |
| Example: QE as "printing Monopoly money" | Misleading | QE buys assets from banks to add liquidity; it is not direct spending money. |
| Example: inflation = "the government prints money" | Oversimplified | One cause among several (demand, supply shocks, expectations). Say "one reason". |
| Example jokes: "his wife left him", DNA typo = "a thing growing where it shouldn't" | Off-style | Gendered stereotype; a cancer punchline. Both fail our punch-direction rules. |

**Lesson from A3:** the briefs' own examples broke their own "accuracy first" rule about once in every three examples. So the style needs a **mandatory fact-check of every joke**, not just every "fact line".

## Part B: the house style

### B1. One sentence
**A sarcastic, high-energy friend roasts a concept's absurd truth until you understand it, then admits they love it.**

> **Creator decision (18 Oct 2026): no deadpan hosting.** The host is expressive and animated, and the first 2–3 s must be a hard hook (energy, a provocative claim, a visual jolt). The deadpan *writing* devices (understatement, mock-official documents) stay; only the *performance* is not deadpan.

### B2. The persona: "the sarcastic friend who knows too much"
- Knows the subject cold; finds it ridiculous; is on the viewer's side (we laugh *together* at the universe, the system, our own habits).
- **Expressive, not deadpan**: bright hook energy, playful eyebrows and a knowing smirk on the punchlines, a quick lean-in on the roast, warm on the toast. Sarcasm is in the words and the timing, not in a flat face.
- **Self-deprecating** where the concept hits us too ("I also have an EMI").
- Indian everyday frame of reference: chai, samosa, EMI, UPI, traffic, family WhatsApp forwards, exam season. Situational jokes that survive translation (not puns), because the voice may be Hindi/Hinglish while the on-screen text is English.

### B3. Structure: the six beats (45–60 s)
| # | Beat | Time | Job | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hook roast** | 0–3 s | Provocative, high-energy claim about the viewer + a true surprising fact ("your battery is breaking up with you, and it's your fault") | Frame 0 already shows the concept as a character + the roast line; the voice starts at 0.0 s, loud and quick; a visual jolt (stamp slam, zoom punch) lands before 1.5 s |
| 2 | **Plain truth** | ~3–9 s | Say what it actually is, straight | One or two plain sentences + a **REAL FACT** stamp; no joke in this beat (alternation) |
| 3 | **Wrong-but-right analogy** | ~9–20 s | A ridiculous but structurally exact comparison | Must pass the mapping test (B5); label it ANALOGY on screen |
| 4 | **Escalation ×3** | ~20–35 s | Rule of three: two expected facts, the third absurd but true | Each step is a real fact; a laugh beat every 5–8 s |
| 5 | **The dark turn** | ~35–45 s | "haha… wait… oh no": the consequence at system or cosmic scale | Benign cues: abstract target, deadpan, distance; dark dial ≤ 3 (B6) |
| 6 | **The toast + callback + send** | last 8–10 s | One sincere line; the opening joke returns; "send this to the friend who…" | Toast is warm and true; CTA asks for a send, not a like |

**Joke placement:** strongest jokes at the cold roast, at the midpoint (escalation peak) and at the callback (matches the conference-talk clusters). Write ~2× the jokes you need, read them to someone, keep only the ones that get a real laugh.

### B4. Joke engines (pick 2–3 per reel, never all)
| Engine | Use it for | Example (checked) |
|---|---|---|
| **Personification** (concept as a toxic coworker, landlord, ex, HR) | Processes with "behaviour" | "Compound interest is the friend who's useless for 20 years and then buys a house." |
| **Mundane × scientific** (the most common humour strategy in science writing, per Cook et al. 2025) | Big science, small life | "Your body is replacing cells right now. Not your bad decisions, though." |
| **Deadpan understatement** | Huge, scary things | "A black hole is a mildly inconvenient place to park." |
| **Mock-official document** (memo, warning label, terms & conditions, performance review, HR complaint) | Rules, risks, fine print | "INFLATION™. Terms: about 4% a year is RBI's target; no refunds." |
| **Literalisation** | Idioms | "'Money burning a hole in your pocket'": the pocket is on fire; then the spending fact. |
| **Rule of three with a twist** | Lists | "Your phone battery hates heat, sitting at 100%, and you." |
| **Feigned ignorance, then correction** (Cunk-style) | Myths | Says the myth with confidence; a **MYTH** stamp slams; the real fact follows within 2 s. Use at most once per reel. |
| **Accurate chart crime** | Numbers | An absurd-looking chart whose numbers are true. |
| **Callback** | The ending | The cold roast returns, now with the viewer in on it. |
| **Self-own** | Softening a hard roast | "Also, I checked my own savings. I'm the samosa." |

### B5. The four truth tests (every joke, not only every fact)
1. **Remove-the-jokes test**: delete every joke; the explanation still makes sense on its own.
2. **Mapping test**: for each analogy, list "part of analogy → part of mechanism". If a part has no match, say where the analogy breaks (that can itself be a joke) or pick another analogy.
3. **Myth test**: check the joke against the myth list (10% of the brain, Newton's apple bonk, wolves change rivers, the observer is a person, goldfish 3-second memory, and so on). Comedy must not spread a myth.
4. **Source test**: every number has a tier-A/B source in the script's fact table, same as all reels.

### B6. The dark dial (how dark, and about what)
| Level | Mood | Allowed targets |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Gentle | "bless its heart" | everyday habits, small inconveniences |
| 2 Sarcastic | "the system is ridiculous" | institutions, incentives, apps, banks, big companies (true claims only) |
| 3 Existential | "the universe does not care" | entropy, the Sun's future, deep time, cells dying on schedule, our insignificance |
| 4 Off limits | — | see the banned list below |

Default for a reel: **2, with one moment at 3** (the dark turn).

**Banned (never a punchline):** suicide/self-harm (Instagram policy and harm); real deaths, disasters, terror, war victims; illness of real people or cancer as a punchline; religion, deities, mythology (incl. our Ramayana reels); caste, region, language groups, gender stereotypes, body shaming, disability; named politicians; private individuals; profanity; sexual content. India context: FIRs over comedy (the 2025 *India's Got Latent* cases) show "the process is the punishment" even when courts later side with the comedian.

### B7. Voice and performance (creator decision: no deadpan)
- **Hook (0–3 s):** the avatar rule R4 applies as written: ENTHUSIASTIC AND ENGAGING, quicker, a confident grin, dead-on at the lens.
- **Body:** expressive sarcastic friend: lively pitch movement, a playful eyebrow and a knowing smirk on each punchline, a short 0.3 s pause *before* the punchline (timing), the punchline lands with a little extra bite, not shouted.
- **Toast:** warm and sincere, a genuine smile. No laugh track, no "haha".

## Part C: production kit (repo assets)

| Need | How we build it |
|---|---|
| **Concept character** | A non-human object mascot (house rule: no cartoon people): the concept as a googly-eyed object (a coin, a battery, a cell, a planet), SVG or a Blender prop; given a name plate ("COMPOUND INTEREST, 72, unbothered"). One mascot per reel; recurring mascots per series are welcome. |
| **Host** | Avatar tile (energetic hook, expressive sarcastic body) or cloned voice; punch-in zoom 1.0 → 1.12 over 0.2 s on punchlines. |
| **Truth markers** | `REAL FACT` stamp (on every fact beat), `ANALOGY` tag (on every analogy), `MYTH` stamp (on any myth said for effect). The lint checks every fact beat has its stamp. |
| **Mock documents** | Memo, warning label, ingredients, terms & conditions, HR complaint form, performance review, parody ad with fine print: DOM components with a typewriter reveal. |
| **Comic timing** | Freeze-frame + record-scratch; hard cut to silence; the punchline word highlighted in the caption; a "beat" of stillness before it. |
| **Sound** | The 959 CC0 UI sounds incl. `rubber` (boings) and `arcade` (fail buzzers); synth record-scratch, cash register, tiny drum hit. Sad trombone at most once per reel. **Music drops out for 0.4 s under each punchline** (silence is the drum). |
| **Charts and numbers** | Odometers, bars, "accurate chart crimes", rough.js red-pen circles. |
| **Real-world proof** | Photos/Unsplash/real stills for the plain-truth beat, so the reel looks like it respects reality. |
| **Visual identity** | Per reel, as always (palette, fonts, components), but the series keeps a constant **truth-marker system** (stamps, tags) so viewers learn to read it. |

### C1. Script template (one row per beat; every joke row must fill "fact carried" and "target")
| t (s) | Beat | Line (voice) | On-screen text | Fact carried (+ source tier) | Engine | Target (punch direction) | Visual | SFX/music |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

### C2. QA checklist for every roast reel (adds to the standing checklists)
1. Every joke row has a fact and a source; no empty "fact carried" cells.
2. Remove-the-jokes, mapping, myth and source tests pass (B5).
3. Every target is on the allowed list; dark dial ≤ 3; nothing on the banned list (B6).
4. Every fact beat shows a `REAL FACT` stamp; every analogy shows `ANALOGY`; every myth for effect is corrected on screen within 2 s.
5. Joke density: a laugh beat every 5–8 s, alternating with plain facts; the strongest jokes at the open, middle and close.
6. Read-aloud test with 2–3 people: cut any joke that gets only a polite smile.
7. Stranger-send test: would someone send this to a friend? If not, sharpen the cold roast and the callback.
8. Performance check on the avatar clips: the first 2–3 s are high-energy and hooky; punchlines get a beat before them and a smirk, not a flat face.
9. Plus every standing rule: no overlaps, no typos, real people shown only when named and licensed, 2-second change rule, mix targets, caption template, disclosure line.

### C3. Series slate (all pass the banned list)
| Format | Episodes |
|---|---|
| **Roast of ___** | Compound Interest · Your Phone Battery · Your Credit Score · The Mitochondria · Sleep Debt · The Snooze Button |
| **Honest Ad** | Inflation™ · "Buy Now Pay Later" · Free Trials · Cashback |
| **HR Complaint** (an organ or gadget complains about you) | Your Spine vs your chair · Your Phone Storage · Your Eyes vs screen time |
| **The Universe Doesn't Care** (dark dial 3) | The Sun's retirement plan · You are mostly empty space · Your atoms are older than the Earth |

## Part D: worked example (script draft, numbers to verify before production)

**"Roast of Compound Interest"** (≈ 50 s; voice Hinglish/English, on-screen English). Mascot: a smug googly-eyed ₹1 coin named "C.I.".

| t (s) | Beat | Line | Fact carried | Engine | Target |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0–3 | Cold roast | "Compound interest: the only thing in your life that works harder the less you touch it." | Compounding grows on its own past growth | Personification | the concept |
| 3–8 | Plain truth | "It means you earn interest on your interest. Not just on the money you put in." | Definition | — (REAL FACT stamp) | — |
| 8–18 | Analogy | "It's that friend who's useless for twenty years, then suddenly buys a house." | Growth is slow early, steep late (exponential) | Analogy (ANALOGY tag) | the concept |
| 18–30 | Escalation ×3 | "₹10,000 at 12% a year: after 10 years, about ₹31,000. After 20, about ₹96,000. After 30… about three lakh. Same ₹10,000. You just waited." | 1.12^10 ≈ 3.11; 1.12^20 ≈ 9.65; 1.12^30 ≈ 29.96 | Rule of three | the concept |
| 30–40 | Dark turn | "Bad news: it also works for your bank. Credit card dues compound too, at rates that make 12% look like charity." | Credit-card interest compounds on unpaid balances (exact rate per card: tier-A from the issuer before use) | Understatement | the banking system |
| 40–50 | Toast + callback + send | "Respect, though. Start early, then leave it alone, like the coin. Send this to the friend who's going to start 'next month'." | Time in the market matters more than amount early on (keep the claim general) | Callback + self-own | ourselves |

Note: the 12% return is an illustration, labelled "ILLUSTRATIVE RETURN, NOT ADVICE" on screen; this is not financial advice and says so in the caption.
