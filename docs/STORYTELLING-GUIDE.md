# Storytelling guide for 60–120 s reels (31 Oct 2026)

Why this exists: creator feedback after `reel-quick-commerce-itna-jaldi` — *"research more and work more on improving storytelling skills; the story should be interesting, engaging and have a good flow; the whole concept must be conveyed; the reel may run over 60 s if the story is gripping."*
This is a **writing method plus a gate**, not a template to fill in. It sits before `script.md`; a reel does not go to mockup until its beat sheet passes section 5.

**Consequence:** length is not the enemy; an *unearned* second 20 is. A 90-second reel is fine if viewers are still pulled forward at second 60, and the only way to build that pull is cause-and-effect plus loops that are closed on schedule.

## 2. The method: "But / Therefore ladder" (BTL)

### 2.1 Choose the story before the facts
Write these four lines first; if any is vague, the topic is not ready.
- **Protagonist (an object or system the viewer owns or knows):** e.g. "India's own GPS", "the photo your uncle forwarded".
- **Want:** what it is trying to do / what we think is true.
- **Obstacle (the antagonist, concrete):** a failing part, a wrong caption, a hidden cost.
- **Question the viewer will carry:** one sentence, asked in the first 5 s, answered by the last 10 s.

### 2.2 Shape (the default arc for ~75–110 s; scale beats, not structure)

| Beat | Share of runtime | Job | Test |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **Hook** | 0–6 s | A surprising, *concrete* claim with a number and a stake, plus the carried question. No greeting, no context. | Could a stranger repeat it in one sentence? |
| **Plant** | 6–14 s | The "normal": what the protagonist is and wants, in 2 sentences, plus **one planted promise** ("at the end I'll show you …") and one object that will return. | Is there something the viewer is now waiting for? |
| **Complication ladder** | 3–4 rungs × 10–15 s | Each rung = **BUT** (something breaks the expectation) → **THEREFORE** (the consequence, which is the next rung's problem). Each rung ends on a mini-question that the next rung answers. Escalate: cost, scale, or surprise rises every rung. | Delete the connector words: if "and then" fits, the rung is a list item, rewrite it. |
| **Midpoint reversal** | around 40–50% | The thing the viewer believed is wrong, shown, not told. | Does the viewer's picture of the topic change here? |
| **Dark moment** | ~70% | The worst the situation gets, with the human or national cost, in one line. A beat of silence. | Is there a pause long enough to feel? |
| **Turn → proof** | 75–90% | The mechanism that resolves it, **shown** (one bespoke visual, labelled honestly), and the explanation lands now because the viewer needs it. | Can the mechanism be described in one sentence? |
| **Payoff + reframe** | last 8–12 s | Return the planted object/promise, reframe the opening image (same picture, new meaning), one honest limit ("what's still unknown"). | Does the last line change how the first line reads? |
| **CTA** | last 3 s | One ask, in the story's own words. | Is it a continuation, not a bolt-on? |

### 2.3 Open-loop ledger (mandatory table in `script.md`)
List every question the script opens, the second it opens and the second it closes. Rules: ≥ 2 loops live at any time after second 10; no loop open > 40 s without a visible sign of progress; **every loop closes**; the long-range promise from the Plant closes in the payoff.

### 2.4 Rhythm and voice (links to `docs/HINGLISH-VOICEOVER-GUIDE.md`)
- Alternate **long explanatory sentence → short punch** (median 9 words, a 3-word sentence after every 2–3 long ones).
- A **reveal gets a pause** (0.8–1.1 s) *after* the key word, never before it.
- One number per beat; the number the viewer must remember is said twice (spoken, then on screen as the voice lands on it).
- One **specific human detail** per ladder (a name of a place, a time, a thing a person did) so it reads as a story about something, not a report.
- One **light moment** (a sly understatement, a callback) so the dark moment has contrast.
- Concrete verbs ("stopped", "stranded", "forwarded"), not abstractions ("challenges", "leverage").

### 2.5 Visual storytelling rules
- The **through-line object** carries the plot: it is on screen when the protagonist wants, breaks at the obstacle, and is rebuilt at the turn (e.g. a ring of four rays that loses one, then closes again).
- **Picture changes on the connector**: every BUT is a visual break (glitch, cut, colour flip), every THEREFORE a push/match-cut. The viewer should feel the connector without hearing it.
- Keep the **proof visual** (2.2 Turn) the most crafted object in the reel; give it the longest hold.
- Honest labels stay (illustrative, slowed ×N, REPORTED).

## 3. Topic filter: does it have a story or only facts?

A topic passes only with all of: (1) a named protagonist the viewer recognises; (2) a real obstacle with a cause; (3) at least **one reversal** a viewer would not guess; (4) a mechanism that can be *shown*; (5) an honest ending (what is solved, what is not); (6) ≥ 3 tier-A/B facts per rung. A topic that is "how X works" without an obstacle is a lesson, not a story: find the failure, the myth, the race or the cost that makes it matter.

## 4. Process (what happens, in order)
1. **Research** to a fact ledger with tiers (A/B/C) and a "do not claim" list. Pull the *chronology* (dated events), not just the mechanism.
2. **Story line** (2.1) in four sentences; one-sentence story test: "*[Protagonist] wanted [want], but [obstacle], therefore [turn], so [payoff].*"
3. **Beat sheet** with connectors written out, rung by rung, with the loop ledger.
4. **Cold read:** spawn a fresh reviewer with *only the script text* and the 7-axis rubric; ask for (a) the connector between every pair of beats, (b) the second they would scroll away, (c) what they think the story is. Fix and repeat until (c) matches the story line.
5. Only then: voice plan, mockup, build.
6. **Retention map** before render: a table of predicted drop-off risks (second, why, fix). After posting, compare with the real retention curve from Insights and write the difference here.

## 5. Gate checklist (a script is not approved until every box is ticked)

- [ ] One-sentence story test passes (protagonist, want, obstacle, turn, payoff).
- [ ] Every adjacent pair of beats joins with **but** or **therefore**; the connectors are written in the beat sheet.
- [ ] Hook has a concrete stake and the carried question by second 5; frame 0 is already the story.
- [ ] A planted promise/object is paid off in the last 12 s; the last line reframes the first.
- [ ] Loop ledger complete; ≥ 2 loops live after 10 s; none open > 40 s; none left unpaid.
- [ ] A reversal around the midpoint; a dark moment with a pause; a light moment.
- [ ] A human detail in every rung; one number per beat.
- [ ] The core concept is explained **once, shown**, at the turn, in one sentence.
- [ ] An honest limit stated before the CTA.
- [ ] Cold-read reviewer retells the story correctly and names no scroll-away point before the last 15 s.
- [ ] Runtime justified: every beat > 12 s changes the viewer's picture or opens a question (otherwise cut).

## 6. Scoring rubric (use for the cold read; ship at ≥ 8 on every axis)
Hook · Causality (but/therefore) · Escalation · Reversal · Clarity of the core concept · Payoff/reframe · Rhythm and voice. Score 1–10 with the second-stamped reason for any score below 8.
