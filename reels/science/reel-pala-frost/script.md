# Pala — "Thermometer ne 4° dikhaya, phir bhi khet mein pala. Kaise?"

Series (proposed, your call): **खेत का विज्ञान · EP 01** (a new tag for village-relatable reels; "Shastra ya Science?" does not fit a weather topic).
Format: vertical 1080×1920 · Hindi/Hinglish voice-over only · Roman captions (plain 40 px) with big **Devanagari** headlines · target 75–80 s · publish **1–5 Dec 2026** (before the cold-wave weeks). Script stage only: no mockup or build yet.

## Why this reel
- **Village stake and season:** rabi crops are in the ground; potato, mustard and lentil are the exposed ones, and late sowing is the riskiest. A warning is only useful before the cold nights.
- **A reversal the viewer can check:** the thermometer is not wrong, it measures a different place from the leaf.
- **Lessons applied from Jatayu and Dev Deepawali:** first surprising fact by 8 s, one promised test planted early and paid off late, the follow ask placed before the last payoff, no spoken "comment karo" prompt (the opinion question goes in the caption), plain Hindi words, a number only where it is sourced.
- **Honesty about the evidence:** Indian state advice and the FAO disagree on smoke. The reel says so and rests its advice on what both support (evening irrigation, covers).

## One-sentence story
On a clear, still winter night the leaf in your field is colder than the air a thermometer reads, so pala can burn your crop while the weather says it is above zero.

## Story test
- **Protagonist:** the leaf in your own field. **Want:** to get through the night. **Obstacle:** the one number you trust, the thermometer, reads the wrong place. **Carried question:** "Chaar degree mein pala kaise?" (asked at 3 s, answered by 30 s).
- **Reversal (≈ 35%):** "Thermometer hawa ka tapman batata hai, patta zameen ke paas hai." **Honest limit:** nobody gave us a number for how much colder the leaf is, so the reel shows it as an illustration and gives the viewer a test.

## Style (Lemo-Opuscar)
- **Chosen: `microscope`** (one continuous change of scale; focus breathes at each step; a single accent colour; every reading sourced or labelled). The reel is one descent: the field at dawn → a row → one leaf → its surface and the frost crystals, and one climb back. "Scale is the camera" is the whole camera plan.
- **Invariants kept:** one continuous scale move per sequence; focus rack at each change of scale; one reading per screen in the accent colour, each stated as measured, sourced or illustrative.
- **Adapted (our rules win):** vertical 1080×1920 at 30 fps; Devanagari headlines with a key-word block; plain 40 px captions (no bar); no gray satellite grade; temperatures are **illustrative**, never presented as measurements; the "thermal view" is labelled ILLUSTRATIVE; the end card carries nerdlogic.co.
- **Runner-up:** `lidar-survey` (a survey of a field as points). Not chosen because the story is about the leaf's heat loss, not geometry.
- **Identity:** pre-dawn steel-blue and graphite ground, frost-white, one warm accent (leaf-orange, used only for heat leaving the leaf) and one cool cyan (cold air only). Fonts to vendor at the mockup stage: a Devanagari display face that is **not** Baloo 2 (Dev Deepawali used it), e.g. Mukta, Hind or Yatra One, with Inter for captions and a mono face for readouts.
