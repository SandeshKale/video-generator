# Bird on the wire — "Chidiya taar par baithi hai, use jhatka kyon nahi lagta?"

Working title; folder `reels/science/reel-bird-on-wire`. Status: script + style + mockup plan (17 Nov 2026). Chosen by the creator from `insights/next-reel-topics-2026-11-17-motion.md` (topic A). Ledger: `research/sources.md`.

## Why this reel
- Brief (creator, 17 Nov): ultra realism **and real motion and animation** (the pala reel was stills with a camera push), a topic a village-living 10th-pass viewer cares about.
- Stake: electric poles are in every village lane; electrocution deaths run to tens of thousands over a decade in NCRB-based reports (tier B, not voiced as a number). One everyday puzzle (the bird) carries the real safety lesson (a second contact, a wet pole, a kite string, a fallen wire).
- Data check (`insights/` report): the account has 6 followers and no audience data, so the pick rests on stake, hook strength and shareability, not proven reach. Not film-linked.

## One-sentence story
A bird sits safely on a live wire because both its feet are at the same voltage, so electricity has no reason to pass through it; the danger is never the wire, it is a second point at a different voltage, and that is exactly what a kite string, a pole or a fallen wire gives you.

## Story test
- Protagonist: the bird on the wire (and the viewer, who is the one who can become the second contact).
- Obstacle: 11,000-volt-class wire; the bird is calm. Why?
- Reversal: the wire is not "safe for birds"; **nothing pushes current through the bird**. Danger comes from the *difference*.
- Showable mechanism: voltage painted as a colour field in the real scene: same colour across the feet, different colours across two wires, or across a wire and the ground.
- Honest ending: the line is always live; the bird is lucky, not immune (big birds do die); you shuffle away from a fallen wire and call the bijli vibhag.

## Script — Hinglish master (written for the ear; ≈ 215 words, ≈ 80 s at ~2.7 words/s)
1. Gyaarah hazaar volt ka taar. Uspar chidiya aaraam se baithi hai. Use jhatka kyon nahi lagta?
2. Log kehte hain: chidiya halki hai. Ya uske par alag hain. Par ye galat hai.
3. Jhatka voltage se nahi lagta. Voltage ke **farq** se lagta hai.
4. Chidiya ke dono pair ek hi taar par hain. Dono jagah voltage ek jaisa. Farq zero ke kareeb. To bijli ko chidiya ke andar se guzarne ki wajah hi nahi milti.
5. Hamne hisaab lagaya: taar mein sau ampere maan lo. Do pair ke beech paanch centimeter ka taar, to farq sirf ek volt ka hazaarwa hissa. Chidiya ke andar se ek microampere ke kareeb. (Ye sirf ek udaharan hai.)
6. Ab ulta socho. Wahi chidiya agar apna pankh doosre taar se laga de, to ek taar par voltage alag, doosre par alag. Ab farq bana, aur bijli chidiya ke sharir se guzar jaati hai.
7. Isiliye taaron ke beech doori rakhi jaati hai. Badi chidiya ke liye khatra zyada hota hai.
8. Ab aap sochiye: aap chidiya nahi ho. Aapke pair zameen par hain. Zameen ka voltage alag, taar ka alag. Bans, lohe ki seedhi, geela khamba, ya dhaatu wala patang ka manjha: ye sab aapko taar se jodte hain, aur farq aapke sharir se guzarta hai.
9. Aur agar taar toot kar gir jaaye? Zameen par gol gol ghere ban jaate hain, har ghere mein alag voltage. Do kadam ke beech bhi farq ho sakta hai.
10. To paas mat jao. Chhote chhote kadam, pair jodkar, door chale jao. Kisi ko bachane ke liye haath mat lagao. Bijli vibhag ko phone karo.
11. Aisi roz ki cheezon ka vigyan chahiye? nerdlogic dot co ko follow karo.
12. Chidiya isliye bachti hai kyunki wo ek hi taar ko chhuti hai. Ek taar, ek voltage, koi farq nahi.

Spoken-text notes: "voltage" and "microampere" are spoken as such; display captions are Roman Hinglish; **no distance number and no helpline number is voiced**; line 8's manjha clause is attributed on screen as REPORTED; line 5 is labelled ILLUSTRATIVE with its assumptions (100 A, 5 cm, 1 kΩ). ## Style (Lemo-Opuscar, standing rule)
- **Chosen: `white-model`, adapted.** Grammar kept: *the scene never changes; only the light does* — voltage is projected as soft coloured light on matter (a sequential ramp: blue = earth, through to hot orange = line voltage), edges soft and wide (no hairline sweeps), a lamp "strikes" to start the layer, house lights return at the end, one reading per layer with a source line, a physical-room sound palette (fan hum, relay, glass notes, silence before the turn). Invariants kept: (1) light is the actor and data is never painted into the material; (2) one hue family per layer, a colour returns only with the same meaning; (3) layers arrive one at a time and the camera moves slowly and continuously.
- **Adapted (our rules and the creator's brief win):** the model is **not white**: the village pole line is a photoreal Cycles render at dusk (the creator's "ultra realism"), and the projector effect is the voltage field laid over real surfaces; vertical 1080×1920 at 30 fps; Devanagari headlines; plain 40 px captions; no cartoon people (the only figure is a brick/voxel boy for the manjha beat); authored camera with no drift.
- **Runner-up:** `microscope` (a continuous scale descent from the pole to the bird's feet to the wire's strands). Not chosen because the story is about a difference between two points, which a colour field shows better than a change of scale.

## Benchmark to beat (pala reel, the previous reel)
Pala: eight still renders, a depth-parallax push and DOM overlays; **nothing in the world moved**. This reel must:
1. **Animate the world itself:** wire sag and sway, a bird that lands, hops, turns its head and flaps (rigged and keyframed, rendered in Cycles in short clips, 12 fps interpolated to 30), grass and a flag moving, a boy running, dust and kite strings swaying. Every scene contains independent motion that is not the camera.
2. **A new technique: a voltage field as light.** A shader that paints potential as a coloured glow over the real render's surfaces using an analytic field (uniform on one wire; a gradient between two wires; rings on the ground around a fallen wire). It moves because the *situation* moves (the wing touching, the boy's bamboo).
3. **A new data object per scene:** a floating millivolt meter across the feet, a body "colour step" gauge, a two-wire bridge diagram, a ring map of ground voltage with two stepping boots, the line's current drawn as pulses running *along* the wire, not through the bird.
4. **Real clip density:** at least three real Cycles-animated clips (landing, wing touch, running boy), not stills.
Honest limit: path-traced animation is slow here, so Cycles clips are short, small (≤ 540×960 rendered, then upscaled with sharpening and film grain) and few; the long moves are authored camera on rendered plates.

## Mockup findings (17 Nov 2026) — `mockup/mockup.mp4` (12 s, silent), `mockup/contact-sheet.png`
Three scenes from three families: (A) a Cycles-animated dusk pole-line clip with current pulses travelling along the wires (emission pulses, a keyframed texture offset), (B) a Cycles rack-focus clip (focus eases from the pulsing wire to a procedural sparrow, with a head turn and tail flick) with a live millivolt meter, (C) a procedural colour-step diagram (potential as light between two wires, current pulses only when the step exists).
- **Measured render cost (4 CPUs, Cycles, OIDN):** wide dusk clip 5.5 s/frame at 540×960, 32 spp; the rack-focus (depth of field) clip 8.0 s/frame at 540×960, 32 spp; a still at 405×720, 24 spp takes ~3 s. A 12 fps clip of 24 frames costs 2–3 minutes, so **an 80 s film at 12 fps is ~960 frames, about 2 h of Cycles at this size**, which is feasible in resumable chunks (interpolated to 30 fps, film grain and sharpening added in post).
- **What works:** real motion in the world (wire pulses, a focus rack, a head turn, tail flick, a travelling field), the dusk light and depth of field read as photographic, and the concept (a colour step is the danger) is clear in scene C even in this crude form.
- **What fails the "ultra-real" bar:** the procedural sparrow reads as a toy (smooth ellipsoid body, no feather structure, disc wings), the establishing wide shot is pale and flat with a bland ground plane and headline text that is unreadable on the bright sky, and the HDRI sun never appears in frame. A photoreal animated bird is **not** achievable with this procedural model; closer shots of the bird must be avoided or replaced.
- **Fix plan (decisions for the creator below):** (1) keep the bird small, backlit and out of focus (the rack-focus pattern) so it reads as a silhouette, and (2) use **real photographs** (CC-licensed house sparrow / crow on a wire, from Wikimedia Commons, credited) as depth-lit planes for any close beat, with the Cycles wire and field in front of and behind them; or (3) ask for a CC0 rigged bird model if one can be sourced; (4) rebuild the wide plate with a lower camera, a real horizon sun, a dirt lane, a house wall and grass so it has foreground, and put headlines on a darker scrim.
