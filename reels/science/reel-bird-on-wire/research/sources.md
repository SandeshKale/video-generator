| 1 | "It's not voltages per se that kill, but voltage differences, which are needed for electricity to flow." A bird on a single line at 35,000 V is safe because its body is at the line's voltage, so no current passes through it. It would be electrocuted if it spread its wings and touched another line at a different voltage; utilities leave plenty of space between cables | **A** | R. | 2 | A downed line can electrify the ground, vehicles and anything in its path; stray voltage spreads in rings, each ring a different voltage; stepping between rings is "step potential" and can electrocute you; do not approach a scene with a downed line; warn others to stay away; stay in a vehicle unless it is on fire, then hop with feet together at least 50 ft | **A** | Illinois Extension, Safe Electricity, "Downed power lines can kill", 1 Jul 2020 (E. ## Not claimed
- The line voltage of "the" village line: 11 kV is a common distribution voltage in India but the reel says "kai jagah gyaarah hazaar volt" (not a measurement, not any specific line); open a DISCOM or CEA page before stating it as a fact.
- Any national death count for electrocution or for kite-string electrocution.
- A human current threshold (IEC 60479) without opening the standard; the reel compares the bird to nothing numerical about a person.
- A helpline number: state DISCOM numbers differ; the script says "bijli vibhag ko phone karo".
- Birds never get hurt: the reel says the opposite (two wires, wire plus pole, a wet pole, big birds).
