## 3. How big can a flying bird be?
| # | Fact | Tier | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | Heaviest living fliers: kori bustard up to ~19 kg (NHM London); wandering albatross up to ~16 kg; Andean condor max ~15 kg, wingspan up to 3.3 m | B | NHM page (snippet), Wikipedia Argentavis/List of largest birds |
| S2 | Argentavis magnificens: ~70-72 kg (later studies; 80-120 kg in older ones), wingspan about 5-6 m (estimates 5.1-7.5 m), 9-6.8 million years ago, Argentina; mostly soared, needed wind or a slope to take off | B | Wikipedia Argentavis (read) |
| S3 | Pelagornis sandersi: wingspan 6.06-7.38 m, 22-40 kg | B | same page, Ksepka 2014 |
| S4 | Birds' wing loading runs 1-20 kg/m2; rule-of-thumb upper limit 25 kg/m2 (Meunier 1951); take-off speed rises with the square root of wing loading | B | Wikipedia Wing loading (read) |
| S5 | Double every length: weight x8, wing area x4, so wing loading x2 | OURS | geometry |
| S6 | An Indian vulture scaled 5x (12 m wings) would weigh ~750 kg with 5x the wing loading | OURS | 6 kg x 125 |
| S7 | Rüppell's vulture holds the height record: 11,300 m, hit an aircraft over Abidjan, 29 Nov 1973 | B | Wikipedia (read) |
Not said: a hard "15 kg limit" (unsourced), "feathers wear out" (single source).

## 4. What makes a bird light and strong (for the exploded view)
| # | Fact | Tier | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| B1 | Skeleton is only about 5% of body weight; many bones are hollow with criss-cross struts | B | Wikipedia Bird anatomy (read) |
| B2 | Plumage weighs 2-3 times as much as the skeleton | B | Wikipedia Feather (read) |
| B3 | Keeled breastbone anchors the flight muscles; pectoralis ~15-25% of body weight, both flight muscles together ~25-40% | B | Bird anatomy |
| B4 | The muscle that raises the wing works through a pulley | B | Bird anatomy |
| B5 | Typically 9 air sacs; air flows one way through the lungs | B | Bird anatomy |
| B6 | Feather: shaft, barbs, barbules with tiny hooks that zip together; made of beta-keratin | B | Wikipedia Feather |
| B7 | No teeth: a light beak instead | B | Bird anatomy |

## 5. India's real vultures
| # | Fact | Tier | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Indian vulture *Gyps indicus*: 5.5-6.3 kg, wingspan 2.2-2.6 m, Critically Endangered since 2002 | B | Wikipedia Indian vulture (read) |
| R2 | Fell ~97% in 10-15 years from the 1990s; other sources say up to 99.9% for some species | B | same; Wildlife Conservation Trust |
| R3 | Cause: diclofenac given to cattle causes kidney failure in vultures that eat the carcass | B | same |
| R4 | India banned veterinary diclofenac in March 2006; sale made punishable in 2008 | B | same |
| R5 | Frank & Sudarshan (Univ. of Chicago / Warwick, American Economic Review): about 100,000 extra human deaths a year, 2000-05, linked to the loss (more feral dogs, rabies) | B | save-vultures.org summary (read) |
| R6 | The breeding centre at Pinjore, Haryana is named the Jatayu Conservation Breeding Centre (BNHS); first captive-bred white-backed vultures released in 2020 (Wikipedia says releases began 2016: conflict, say "in recent years") | B | inkl / Wikipedia |
| R7 | About 5,000-15,000 Indian vultures remain in the wild | B | Wikipedia |
| R8 | Jatayu sculpture, Chadayamangalam, Kerala: 200 ft long, 150 ft wide, 70 ft high; described as the largest bird sculpture | B | Wikipedia (cites The Quint) |
