In the 1.5 seconds it takes to read a line, about 100 billion solar neutrinos pass through your thumbnail: 65 billion per square centimetre every second, made in the Sun's core. Earth doesn't stop them, so at night they come up through the planet and through your bed.

15 km up, cosmic rays smash into the air and rain down muons. A muon lives 2.2 microseconds, enough for about 660 m, yet it reaches the ground because its clock runs slow at near light speed (Einstein's time dilation). About one crosses your palm every second.

Inside you, about 4,000 potassium-40 atoms decay every second, the radioactivity of 250+ bananas. Earth sweeps up about 44 tonnes of space dust a day, arriving at 11–72 km/s. And you are never still: 1,670 km/h spinning (about 1,580 in Mumbai), 107,000 km/h around the Sun, 828,000 km/h around the galaxy.
