# Dev Deepawali — "Devta bhi Diwali manate hain. Par yahi raat kyon?"

Series: **Shastra ya Science?** (episode badge on frame 1; number set by the creator, this is the first reel scripted under the name).
Publish: **21–22 Nov 2026** (the event is Tue 24 Nov). Master cut for Instagram ≈ 75–78 s as written (≈ 68 s with the trim list); YouTube cut ≈ 95–100 s. Script stage only: no mockup or build yet.

## Why this reel
- Account data: the Ramayana-linked reels are the best performers, and Jatayu's flat middle showed that "katha → real thing → honest limit → twist" holds people who stay.
- Playbook: Question > Verdict > News; payoff or question in the first 3 s in voice **and** text; a mini-payoff near 15 s; a late twist; one sendable take-away; a real opinion question; a follow contract.
- Jatayu lessons applied: first surprising fact by 8 s, no spoken comment prompt, 70–75 s, the follow ask sits before the last payoff, something the viewer can do themselves.
- Timeliness: the reel is useful for three days before the night, and the take-away happens on the night itself.

## Story test
- **One sentence:** The gods' Diwali falls on 24 November because of a story about one arrow, but the night itself is picked by the sky: fifteen days after the moonless Diwali the Moon is full, and this year it sits right beside the star cluster that gave Kartik its name.
- **Protagonist:** one diya on the viewer's own roof. **Want:** to know why this night. **Obstacle:** the old story gives no date. **Carried question:** "Par yahi raat kyon?" (asked at 7 s, answered at 58 s).
- **Reversal (≈ 45%):** the Mahabharata's telling has no date at all. **Honest limit:** the Moon is not this close every year, and a full Moon hides the cluster unless you cover it.

## Style (Lemo-Opuscar)
- **Chosen: `paper-lantern`** (layered paper-cut lightbox lit from behind). The story is literally about light switching on: lamps and stars are holes that burn through paper, the three cities are three depth layers that must line up for one ray, and the last shot steps out to the real lamp on a roof ("the box as keepsake").
- **Invariants kept:** every element is a flat sheet in one box with a back-to-front value ladder and cast shadows; light cues are the big motion (source, then layers, then windows); hard actions land on spoken words.
- **Adapted (our rules win):** vertical 1080×1920, 30 fps; no hand-held micro-drift (camera locked between authored moves); captions plain 40 px Roman, not a serif strip; Indian vocabulary only (ghat steps, shikhara, jaali, rangoli), none of the style's Chinese motifs; no figures at all: Shiva is shown only as a bow and a ray, devas only as lamps (respect rule, 🏮 flag).
- **Runner-up:** `shadow-puppet` (the playbook's kathputli idea). Not chosen because it depends on human-like figures and a deity's likeness.
- **Identity:** Kartik-night indigo `#0B1026` → river teal `#12403f` → paper ivory; marigold `#F5A623` = diya light (the only saturated colour), cool white `#dfe9ff` = moon and stars. Fonts to vendor: **Tiro Devanagari Hindi** (headlines), **Baloo 2** (numbers and tags), Inter (captions). Neither dark-lamp palette used before (chat-not-diary is umber/crimson).
