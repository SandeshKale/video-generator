Google just put four AI chips in orbit. 🛰️

On Oct 1, a refrigerator-sized prototype satellite launched from California on a SpaceX rideshare mission. About 61 minutes after liftoff it was released into orbit, and Google says it has made contact and everything is working.

Why bother? AI is running into a power wall. The same week, Amazon locked up 690 megawatts of nuclear power for 20 years. Google's moonshot, Project Suncatcher, asks: what if the compute went where the sun never sets?

Here's what the test is really about:
☀️ A dawn-dusk orbit keeps the panels in near-constant sunlight
🔬 Before launch, Google hit its Trillium TPUs with a proton beam while they ran AI. They survived more radiation than a five-year mission delivers
🛰️ The long-term plan: 81 satellites in a 1 km cluster, 650 km up, 100–200 m apart, working like one computer
💸 Google's math: if launch falls to about $200/kg, a kilowatt in orbit costs roughly $810 a year, comparable to what data centers pay for power. Its own guess for that: around 2035
🔥 The catch: no air in space, so heat can only radiate away. This launch is a survival test, not a working data center

Over the coming weeks Google will gather data on radiation, heat and speed. That decides whether the other 80 ever go up.
