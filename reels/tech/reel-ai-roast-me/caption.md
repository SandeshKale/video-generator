Over 310,000 people did the exact same thing this month: screenshotted their own Instagram feed, handed it to an AI, and asked it to roast them.

Not "review me." Not "give me feedback." Roast me. Destroy me. Be brutal.

And the results are savage — captions mocking your "brand deal with beige" aesthetic, your hobbies, your travel photos, your caption game. People are posting the burns like trophies.

Here's the part that's actually interesting: why does this feel good instead of just mean?

Because there's no relationship on the line. When a friend roasts you, there's a social cost either way — they might mean it, you might overreact, it might change how you see each other. When an AI roasts you, none of that exists. There's nothing to lose by hearing the truth from something that has no stake in your friendship. That's catharsis, not cruelty — and psychologists have language for exactly why honesty lands easier when it comes from someone (or something) with nothing invested in how you take it.

But here's the catch, and it's worth sitting with for a second: it's not actually reading you. It's pattern-matching your captions, your photo choices, your pixels — the same jagged, sometimes-brilliant, sometimes-clueless mechanism behind every other AI trend. The roast is real. The read isn't as deep as it feels.

So enjoy it. Screenshot your feed, ask for the roast, laugh at the burn. Just don't let a pattern-matcher grade your self-worth.
