# Instagram Caption — "The Price Cut That Didn't Matter"

OpenAI just cut GPT-6 prices in half. On paper, that's the story: Luna at $0.10/$0.50 per million tokens, Sol at $2/$10 — both 50% cheaper than before. Easy win, right?

Except the same week, Claude Opus 5.5 dropped — and developers didn't switch to the cheaper model. They switched back to the pricier one.

Here's why: the intelligence gain on GPT-6-Sol over its predecessor is minor — "clearly a step behind" the new Opus model, per this week's Ben's Bites newsletter. One builder put that price cut to the test, pointing GPT-6-Sol at a real, unsupervised task: build a Duolingo-style marketing course. Four hours later, it had a "pretty decent" first draft — and had burned 22% of a $100/month Codex plan doing it.

Meanwhile, Opus 5.5 beat Fable 5.1 on benchmarks, came in cheaper than Opus 5, bumped Claude Code's five-hour limits by 20%, and added a banked rate-limit reset. The newsletter's own writer put it plainly: "My go-to agent changed to Claude Code with this release."

The lesson isn't "OpenAI bad, Anthropic good." It's that a 50% discount doesn't save you money if the model burns 4x the tokens getting to a worse answer. Efficiency beat the discount. 22% of a monthly budget on one task in four hours is the number that actually matters here — not the sticker price.
