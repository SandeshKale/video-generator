# Reel script — "Your AI chat isn't a diary" (draft v1, 2026-10-08)

Format 9:16, 1080×1920@60, ~75 s, Kokoro `am_adam` base ≈1.1, no filler clips, hook payoff by ~3 s.
**Disclosure for the user:** the story involves Anthropic (the maker of Claude, the model that wrote this script). The script therefore sticks to reported facts, attributes every claim, never defends or attacks either side, and does not name the private individual.

## Why this topic
- **Biggest tech story of the week by traffic:** TechSpot's trending list had it at ~745k "hotness" (Oct 5–8); also Tom's Hardware, Slashdot, Futurism, Yahoo, Business Today, WINK News.
- **Personal stakes for every chatbot user** ("is my chat private?"), not a lab-vs-lab fight. Different register from the last agent reels.
- **Concrete dates, a statute, a policy quote, a transparency-report detail** → a bespoke visual per beat (timeline, case-file, policy clause, counter).
- **Share trigger:** "send this to anyone who vents to a chatbot."
- Handled sensitively: it is about *what the policy and process are*, with the individual unnamed, "charged, not convicted", and a balanced closing question.

## Voice-over (≈210 words, ~75 s)
1. She used an AI chatbot like a diary. Then deputies showed up at her door.
2. Here's what's reported. September twenty-sixth, a Florida woman allegedly wrote she'd "shoot up" a sheriff's office. The next day, a message allegedly mentioned a new gun.
3. Anthropic says it monitors chats for threatening content. Serious flags can reach a human reviewer. Here, the team reported it to police.
4. September thirtieth: a felony charge for a written mass-shooting threat. Arraignment is November second. An allegation, not a conviction.
5. First time? No. Reports say at least two other chats reached police since August. San Antonio, a man arrested. San Francisco, no charges.
6. Not just one company. OpenAI has said chats about harming others can go to human reviewers, and imminent threats may go to police.
7. Anthropic's privacy policy, effective September tenth, allows disclosure when it believes that's reasonably necessary to prevent serious harm.
8. Its transparency report shows zero emergency requests from police in late twenty twenty-five. But it doesn't count reports the company makes on its own.
9. The sheriff says what you share with AI is "never truly anonymous." What we don't know: how often chats get flagged.
10. So, a chatbot isn't a diary. Safer, or watched? Tell me below. Follow for the next AI story.

## Fact table (confidence tiers)
**A — reported by several outlets (Tom's Hardware, Slashdot citing court records, Yahoo, Futurism citing WINK News, Business Today)**
- Lee County (Bonita Springs), Florida. A woman's Claude chat entry dated **Sept 26** allegedly said she would "shoot up" the Lee County Sheriff's Office; a **next-day** message allegedly mentioned a new gun.
- Anthropic's systems flagged the chat; it was escalated to a **human reviewer**, who decided to report to law enforcement.
- Detained at home "without incident"; an LCSO intelligence detective took over.
- **Felony charge dated Sept 30**, Florida Statute **836.10** (written/electronic threat of mass shooting/terrorism; second-degree felony). Booking entry: "THREAT-TERRORISTIC-STATE OFFENSES-WRITTEN/ELEC THREAT MASS SHOOTING/TERRORISM ACT." **Arraignment scheduled Nov 2** (Yahoo). The arrest report is **not yet in the court file**; charges are allegations.
- Sheriff Carmine Marceno to WINK News: users share with AI is **"never truly anonymous"**; he said she described using Claude like a "diary" and apparently did not realise employees could read chats (Futurism citing WINK).
- Anthropic's privacy policy, **effective Sept 10**: disclosure to law enforcement where it has a good-faith belief it is "reasonably necessary to … prevent serious harm to any person or to property."
- Anthropic's government-requests report (H2 2025) uses a narrower emergency standard ("danger of death or serious physical injury to a person"), lists **zero** emergency requests from law enforcement for that period, **but does not count referrals Anthropic makes on its own** (Tom's Hardware/Slashdot).
- No on-record Anthropic comment in the coverage seen.
**B — single outlet (Yahoo/Tom's Hardware); label "reports say"**
- "At least the third" such chat to reach police since August: **San Antonio, Aug 11** (queries about shooting at a nearby elementary school; 22-year-old man arrested on a felony terroristic-threat charge, police alerted via the FBI) and **San Francisco, Aug 14** (user threatened Anthropic's CEO, said he had bought an AR-15; Anthropic notified police; no arrest or charge).
- OpenAI's stated policy (Aug 2025 blog as reported by many outlets, not opened directly): conversations about harming others go to a specialised human review pipeline; **only imminent threats of serious physical harm to others may be referred to police**; self-harm is not referred.
**C — do NOT state as fact**
- That the threat was credible / that she would have acted (not established).
- Her name or address (private individual; reports differ on details; not needed).
- How often chats are flagged, how the classifier works, how many are reviewed.
- Anything about OpenAI's Tumbler Ridge case or other uses of ChatGPT in violence (different story, WSJ-sourced, contested).
- That Anthropic "reads all chats" — reporting says automated flagging first, human review for serious flags.

## Do NOT say
- Her name, town beyond "Florida", or any speculation about her mental health.
- "Arrested for what she said to an AI" as a headline frame without the allegation context; say **"charged"** and **"allegedly"**.
- "AI companies spy on you" / "Claude reports you" as blanket claims. Use "flagged, human review, reported".
- That Anthropic's policy is unusual — OpenAI has a comparable stated process.
- Legal conclusions ("this is legal/illegal").
