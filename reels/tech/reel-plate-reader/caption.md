Somebody's keeping notes on your car. 🚗📷

On your way to work there's a small gray camera on a pole. You've never once looked up at it. It doesn't film you: it reads your plate, then writes down the time, the place, and which way you were headed. Do that all over town and it isn't a snapshot anymore. It's a diary of your week.

Police say that diary finds stolen cars and missing people, and they're not wrong. But who gets to read it? This summer a free site let drivers type in their plate and see whether anyone had searched it. Some people got a yes. Reportedly, the company then trimmed what its logs show: fewer names, fewer reasons.

So people stopped asking nicely. NPR counted cameras vandalized in at least 36 states, and by one watchdog's count more than 100 towns have switched cameras off or cancelled contracts. Neighbors who want every stolen car found. Neighbors who want one day without a diary. Both think they're being sensible.
