## Pick: "The robot didn't fight. A gamer did." — the human-vs-humanoid cage fight (REK, San Francisco)

Why it works: the most-shared tech clip of the week, instantly visual (a person in a cage with humanoids), relatable misconception ("the AI robots are coming for us"), and a twist that explains what the clip *actually* shows: the humanoids were piloted in real time, AI only kept them upright. Few numbers, lots of motion — fits the cartwheel-robot register.

### Facts (tiered; A = ≥2 outlets, B = one outlet, C = do not state)
- A: Organiser Robot Entertainment Kombat (REK), SF; billed as "the first cage fight between a human and a humanoid robot". Content creator Frankie LaPenna fought **three** robots: a smaller Unitree G1, then two modified EngineAI T800s (Fox News via CyberGuy/Lifezette; SFist: 3 humanoids).
- A: **Human pilots** drive the robots in real time with **VR gear or a gamepad**; **AI handles balance and trained movement** (REK technology partner Reflex Arc, as reported by Fox News/CyberGuy/Lifezette).
- A: LaPenna was knocked down in a bout; hand injury from a punch; one robot's head came off (per REK CEO Cix Liv). He wore motorcycle-style protective gear (REK required it).
- B: REK says the modified T800s can kick with **up to 850 lb of force** (REK claim via Fox News). Standard T800 ≈ 5'8", 165–187 lb (EngineAI listing); REK's modified ≈ 6 ft.
- B: Reflex Arc says REK's first simulator championship produced **3,670 matches**; winners flew to SF to pilot real robots at REK2 in August.
- B: Event Sept 18, 2026 (SFist). California State Athletic Commission cease-and-desist **Sept 30** (SFist, via Liv's X post): bars REK from "holding, promoting, or advertising any boxing or mixed martial arts contest match or exhibition" involving humans in California without prior approval.
- B: The YouTube video had **8.2M views** when SFist published (Oct 7). REK claims LaPenna won 2 of 3 bouts; SFist disputes it → say "REK says", or skip.
