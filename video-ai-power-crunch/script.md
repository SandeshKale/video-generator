# The Power Crunch — who pays for AI's electricity? (landscape, draft script v1)

Format: 16:9, 1920×1080@60, ~7:00 target, v2 pipeline (photoreal depth-parallax backgrounds + bespoke foreground graphics), brand CTA with profile photo + @sandesh.explains. Sequel to *The AI Bubble Machine*, which promised a power-crisis episode.

## Hook (first 15 s)
> Everyone talks about AI chips. Nvidia. GPUs. The waiting lists. But the real bottleneck isn't silicon. It's a transformer. A wire. A power plant that doesn't exist yet.

Open loops planted early and paid off later: (1) *who covers the gap while power catches up?* → your bill (scenes 12–18); (2) *the twist most people miss* → the grid has room if AI learns to blink (19–20); (3) *"look up"* → Google's orbital TPUs (21–22).

### s04 · photo: `construction-crane` · eyebrow `// 18 MONTHS VS 2032`

### s05 · photo: `outlet-closeup` · eyebrow `// YOU CAN'T STOCKPILE IT`

- And unlike chips, you can't stockpile electricity.
- Every watt a data center pulls, a power plant has to make in that very second.

Foreground: live load-vs-generation balance meter (must match every second) · battery icon crossed out · 24-hour demand curve · outlet/wire macro inset

### s06 · photo: `server-hall` · eyebrow `// 4.4% → UP TO 12%`

- First, how big is this?
- In twenty twenty-three, American data centers used about one hundred seventy-six terawatt-hours. Four-point-four percent of all the electricity in the country.
- Berkeley Lab says that could hit between six-point-seven and twelve percent by twenty twenty-eight.

Foreground: big counter 176 TWh · stacked bar 2023 vs 2028 range (325–580 TWh) · US share donut 4.4% → 6.7–12% · LBNL source tag

### s07 · photo: `globe-grid` · eyebrow `// 415 → 945 TWH BY 2030`

- Globally, the International Energy Agency expects data centers to roughly double, to nine hundred forty-five terawatt-hours by twenty thirty.
- The United States alone adds about two hundred forty.

Foreground: world map with regional growth bubbles (US +240, China +175, EU +45, JP +15) · doubling bar · IEA source tag · animated 'x2' stamp

### s08 · photo: `reactor-hall` · eyebrow `// TECH BUYS NUCLEAR`

- That's why big tech is suddenly in the nuclear business.
- Microsoft is paying to restart Three Mile Island's unit one. Eight hundred thirty-seven megawatts. Twenty-year contract. Back online in twenty twenty-seven.
- Everybody's shopping for electrons.

Foreground: Microsoft + Constellation lockup · Crane (TMI-1) inset · 837 MW dial · 2027 restart countdown · 'everybody's shopping' logo wall (Amazon/Microsoft/Meta/Google) with electron icons

### s09 · photo: `transformer-yard` · eyebrow `// DELIVERY IS THE PROBLEM`

- But buying power is the easy part. Delivering it is the problem.
- Industry estimates put big transformer lead times at three to five years. Switchgear is sold out into twenty twenty-eight.
- And the grid connection line? One to two years, minimum.

Foreground: transformer cross-section illustration with lead-time bar (3–5 yrs) · switchgear 'SOLD OUT → 2028' stamp · interconnection queue clock (1–2 yrs) · substation inset

### s10 · photo: `gas-turbine` · eyebrow `// 116 GW OF TURBINES`

- So the industry is turning to gas turbines.
- GE Vernova's backlog hit one hundred sixteen gigawatts this summer.
- It's now taking reservations for twenty thirty-one. Twenty thirty-one. For a data center someone wants next year.

Foreground: turbine inset + 116 GW stacked backlog bar · '2031' calendar page flipping · GE Vernova customer split 80/20 · price-per-kW ticker

### s11 · photo: `gas-plant-night` · eyebrow `// OFF THE GRID, ON GAS`

- Some companies are skipping the grid entirely.
- Meta's Louisiana campus is getting two and a half gigawatts of dedicated gas plants, on a fifteen-year contract.
- And xAI ran dozens of mobile gas turbines in Memphis just to get online fast.

Foreground: Meta Hyperion 2.5 GW / 15-yr contract card · xAI Memphis turbine row (animated icons) · 'behind the meter' schematic: plant → campus bypassing the grid

### s12 · photo: `suburb-dusk` · eyebrow `// WHO PAYS: PJM'S 13 STATES`

- Now the part that hits your wallet.
- PJM runs the grid across thirteen states, from Virginia to Illinois. Every year, it auctions future power capacity.

Foreground: tile-map of the 13 PJM states lighting up · capacity auction gavel animation · MW-day price ticker · Virginia→Illinois route line

### s13 · photo: `auction-floor` · eyebrow `// $29 → $329 → $333`

- For the twenty twenty-four delivery year, capacity cost twenty-nine dollars a megawatt-day.
- For twenty twenty-six? Three hundred twenty-nine.
- The latest auction, for twenty twenty-seven, came in at three thirty-three. The price cap.

Foreground: 10× staircase chart ($28.92 → $329.17 → $333.44) · cap line · 'CAP' stamp · counting price badge

### s14 · photo: `meter-closeup` · eyebrow `// $9.3B ON EVERYONE'S BILL`

- One analysis blames data centers for sixty-three percent of one year's increase. Nine point three billion dollars, spread across everyone's bills.
- In western Maryland, capacity costs alone were estimated to add about eighteen dollars a month to a typical home. About sixteen in Ohio.

Foreground: utility-bill receipt with line items: capacity charge $16–18/mo highlighted (MD $18, OH $16) · 63% data-center pie slice · $9.3B counter

### s15 · photo: `grid-control-room` · eyebrow `// THE CAP HIDES $530`

- And that cap is hiding something.
- Without it, one estimate says the price would have been closer to five-thirty.
- Worse, PJM came up six thousand six hundred megawatts short of its own reliability target.
- The grid didn't just get expensive. It got thin.

Foreground: two price bars: actual $333 vs. ~$530 uncapped · reliability gauge: 6,625 MW short (reserve margin 20% → ~14.8%) · alarm light · 'THIN GRID' stamp

### s16 · photo: `empty-lot-sunrise` · eyebrow `// $6.2B FOR BUILDINGS THAT DON'T EXIST`

- Here's the uncomfortable part.
- Of that sixteen-point-four billion dollar auction, about six point two billion was tied to data centers that haven't been built yet.
- Forecasts, not buildings. If some of those projects never happen, the capacity still got paid for.
- Utilities call it phantom load. Ratepayers call it a bill.

Foreground: auction pie 16.4B with 6.2B hatched 'not yet built' · empty-lot photo with ghost data-center outline fading in · 'PHANTOM LOAD' stamp

### s17 · photo: `power-lines-dawn` · eyebrow `// 267% — BUT WHOLESALE`

- Bloomberg checked twenty-five thousand pricing points across the grid.
- Near data centers, wholesale power was up as much as two hundred sixty-seven percent in five years.
- That's wholesale, not your bill. But seventy-three percent of the spots where prices rose sit within fifty miles of major data center activity.

Foreground: US node heat-map dots (25,000 points) with 73% within 50 mi of data centers · '+267%' badge with 'wholesale ≠ bill' caveat tag · radius ring animation

### s18 · photo: `capitol-dusk` · eyebrow `// 417–3`

- Voters noticed.
- September sixteenth, the House passed the Ratepayer Protection Act. Four seventeen to three.
- Twenty-five states now have large-load tariffs. They typically make data centers sign long contracts and pay minimum bills, so the cost doesn't land on your meter. Iowa froze one utility's home rates for five years.
- Companies are responding too. Amazon pledged a billion dollars to the communities that host its data centers, over five years.

Foreground: vote tally bar (417 vs 3) · Capitol inset · bill name card · 25-state tile map filling in (large-load tariffs) · Iowa 5-yr freeze tag · Amazon $1B community pledge card

### s19 · photo: `wind-solar-field` · eyebrow `// 76 GW IF AI BLINKS`

- But here's the twist most people miss.
- A Duke University study found the grid could absorb seventy-six gigawatts of new demand, about ten percent of national peak, if data centers agreed to cut back a quarter of one percent of the time.
- That's roughly twenty-two hours a year. The average pause, under two hours.

Foreground: demand-curve with the 0.25% worst hours shaded; data-center load dips (animated) · 76 GW = 10% peak gauge · ~22 h/yr clock · avg pause 1.7 h

### s20 · photo: `server-aisle-blue` · eyebrow `// CAN AI LEARN TO BLINK?`

- The grid has room. It just doesn't have room on the hottest, coldest, worst afternoons.
- So the real fight is this. Will AI learn to blink?

Foreground: two-state toggle animation (RUN / PAUSE) on server rack · grid stress thermometer · 'hottest afternoon' sun icon · question card
