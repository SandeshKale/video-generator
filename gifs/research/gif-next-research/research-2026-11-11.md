## A. "Your retries are the second outage" (recommended)
**Architecture shown:** a 4-tier request path (browser/SDK → frontend → backend → database), the retry multiplication per tier (1 → 4 → 16 → 64), the database capacity ceiling and brown-out, then the four brakes: **one retrying layer, retry budget (token bucket), jittered backoff, load shed (503) + circuit breaker**.
**Relatable hook:** every engineer has written `retry(3)` and every team has layered it.
**Metaphor options:**
1. **The splitting tower (recommended).** A cross-section of a four-floor building, the request path as floors. One glowing packet (a user click) enters at the top. Each floor that waits too long for a reply fires 3 more packets, so 1 → 4 → 16 → 64 packets cascade down the stairwells. The basement (the database) has a capacity gauge with a hard line at 100%. When it redlines it browns out, and the retries keep it down after the original cause is gone (the QPS chart beside the tower: a short blip, then a long retry plateau). Act 2: four brakes are bolted onto the building one by one, each visibly cutting the plateau (64 → 4 attempts; token bucket draining; arrival times fanning out; 503 sign and a breaker switch), until the basement recovers.
2. **The echo chamber:** a hall of mirrors where each failed bounce spawns copies of the ray (more abstract, fewer real components).
3. **The stadium exit:** crowds retrying the same gate in sync (good for jitter, weaker for layering).
**Ticker (real, labelled):** `GITHUB · 17 AUG 2026 · 7 H 47 M · "CLIENT-SIDE RETRY LOOP"`, `GOOGLE CLOUD · US-WEST1 · 20 AUG · "CASCADING RETRIES"`, `AWS · JITTER: −50% CALLS AT 100 CLIENTS`, `GOOGLE SRE · 4 ATTEMPTS^3 = 64`.
