## Post (recommended)

Someone sent you a polished AI document. It looks finished. It isn't. Now fixing it is your job.

Researchers call it "workslop": AI-generated work that looks good but doesn't move the task forward. Follow the numbers in the GIF (BetterUp Labs + Stanford Social Media Lab, 1,150 US full-time workers, published in HBR in Sept 2025):

1. It arrives from everywhere. Of the workslop people received, 40% came from a peer, 18% from a direct report, 16% from a manager.
2. You have to open it to know. The headings, bullets and a chart all look right. The figures aren't checked and the next steps say TBD.
3. About 15.4% of the work content people received fit the description.
4. Fixing one took nearly 2 hours on average. Two of them is half your day.
5. The researchers put the "invisible tax" at $186 per employee per month, which is over $9M a year for a 10,000-person company.

The part that stuck with me: the cost doesn't fall on the person who hit "generate". It lands on the one who has to work out what's missing.

A note on the GIF: the documents, the timings and the 2-in-12 ratio are schematic. The numbers are the study's, via a published summary. This is a 2025 study, not new data.

Before you forward AI output, what's your own check? Mine: can I explain every line without the tool open?

## First comment (sources, how to read it, caveats)

Sources and how to read the GIF:
- Study: "workslop" research by BetterUp Labs and the Stanford Social Media Lab, published in Harvard Business Review (Sept 2025): 1,150 full-time US workers surveyed. Figures used: 15.4% of work content received, nearly 2 hours per incident, $186 per employee per month, more than $9M a year for a 10,000-person company, senders 40% peers / 18% staff to managers / 16% managers to staff. I read these through The Decoder's summary (the-decoder.com, 26 Sept 2025), not the full paper, so check the original for method details.
- These are self-reported survey estimates from a 2025 study, not new data. Later 2026 surveys exist (for example a Zety survey of 1,000 US workers, January 2026) but they use different methods and I did not mix their numbers in.
- The GIF's documents, timings and the 2-hollow-in-12 ratio are schematic, drawn to match the 15.4% figure. "2 hours each" is the study average, not a rule.
- The "my check" line in the post is my own habit, not a finding.
- Disclosure: I used Claude to help research and draft this post and to build the animation.

If you've received workslop, who did it come from: a peer, a report or your manager?
