# Next LinkedIn GIF: topic research (2026-10-15)

Method: web searches on trending LinkedIn topics, 2026 algorithm signals, this week's AI/work news. Aggregator "trending" pages were stale (Taplio's page dated 13 Oct showed an old snapshot; LinkPost's data is June). So topics below are chosen from sourced studies and news that are relatable, not from a live trend feed. Nothing here is verified against LinkedIn's own analytics.

## What the format needs (from last GIF's research, still valid)
Feed GIF <= 5 MB, <= 400 frames; saves and dwell time outweigh likes; a walkthrough with a labelled, save-worthy end state beats a plain diagram; closing question that asks for a judgement; links go in the first comment. Next GIF must be **horizontal 1600x900**, a **new palette**, lead with a **visual metaphor**, ~25 independently moving parts, ≤5 numbered items, legible text (>= 18 px at 1600 wide).

## Candidate topics (ranked)

### A. "Workslop": polished AI work that someone else has to fix (best sourced, most relatable)
- BetterUp Labs + Stanford Social Media Lab, published in HBR (Sept 2025): 1,150 full-time US workers; **15.4%** of work content received fits the description; **nearly 2 hours** to deal with each incident; an "invisible tax" of **$186 per employee per month** (> $9M/yr for 10,000 workers); sources: coworkers **40%**, staff-to-manager **18%**, manager-to-staff **16%**. (The Decoder summary, read 15 Oct; tier B for the numbers, study is tier A but I read a summary, not the paper.)
- 2026 follow-ups are single-vendor surveys (Zety Jan 2026, 1,000 US workers: 55% received it from a manager, 85% say it lowers trust). Use only as a footnote, attributed.
- Why it works on LinkedIn: everyone has received a long, confident, empty AI doc. Pure relatability, a number to save, and a "who sends it" split that invites comments.

### B. "AI doesn't reduce work, it intensifies it" (HBR, 9 Feb 2026, Ranganathan and Ye, Berkeley Haas)
- Secondary coverage: about 200 employees at a US tech firm over eight months; faster pace, broader task scope, work extended into more hours; time saved got reinvested, not returned. I could not read the full HBR text (paywall), so treat design details as tier C until read.
- Why it works: the "productivity paradox" is the most shared work-AI theme of 2026. Weaker as a GIF because the data is soft.

### C. The bot-applicant arms race (AI applies, AI screens)
- One syndicated Pangram Labs article (30 Mar 2026): applications up 45% YoY on LinkedIn, 17% of managers saw deepfakes in interviews (ResumeGenius, 1,000 managers), white-text prompt injection on a resume (NYT, per the article). Single source, March: tier C until confirmed.
- Why it works: LinkedIn-native. Risk: stale and single-sourced.

### Rejected
- LinkedIn layoffs (May 2026, ~5%): old, and "AI isn't the reason" muddies the story.
- This week's agent news (Shopify agentic commerce, Cresta Deputy Agent, Anthropic's Scion): vendor announcements, not relatable.
- A fresh viral-post hook list: no verified source, so I did not invent one.

## Recommendation
A (workslop). Sourced, evergreen-but-timely, universal, and it gives a clean data object: a customs/inspection desk where polished-looking documents arrive, get opened, and turn out hollow.
